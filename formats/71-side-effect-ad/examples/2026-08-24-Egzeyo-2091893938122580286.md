## Full text

> when ai took off, i realized pretty quickly we weren't going to win by fighting it.
> 
> (not sure if this is interesting to anyone here, but our clients are getting surprisingly excited about this)
> 
> some are already buying extra credits. one client basically uses it as his on-brand facebook creative machine.
> 
> the idea came from something we kept seeing after finishing projects.
> 
> we'd build the brand, website, creatives, design system, everything.
> 
> then the client would inevitably open chatgpt, upload a few screenshots and try to extend the brand on their own.
> 
> which is completely fair.
> 
> the problem wasn't that they were using ai.
> 
> the problem was that the ai had almost none of the context we had when designing the brand in the first place.
> 
> so instead of fighting that behavior, we built around it.
> 
> after we finish a project, clients can get access to their own marketing studio.
> 
> it has their actual brand assets, design system, visual references and project context already built in, with different models available depending on what they're trying to make.
> 
> they can use it to explore a new ad creative, draft some ugc, test a campaign idea or even visualize a new section of their website before asking us to build it properly.
> 
> the underlying models aren't the interesting part.
> 
> models are becoming commodities.
> 
> context isn't.
> 
> the useful part is giving the client ai that starts from the same system we designed for them, instead of starting from a blank chat window and a logo upload.
> 
> it doesn't replace the agency work.
> 
> it gives clients a much better way to extend, experiment with and communicate around the work we've already done.
> 
> and, as a nice side effect, considerably less ai slop.
