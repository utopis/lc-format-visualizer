## Full text

> Here are the three types of contests that you can run on TikTok Shop:
> 
> #1. A view-based contest where you incentivize people for getting a certain number of views on a video. It might be $1 per 1,000 views or something like that.
> 
> This is my least favorite type of contest because it's so easy for people to game. They can bot views very easily. 
> 
> Of course, there are ways to know if views are botted but most brands just don't have the time and resources to sort through all the botted submissions. Would highly recommend not doing this type of contest.
> 
> #2. A GMV-based contest where you give people additional rewards for hitting GMV incentives. 
>  
> This is like the OG Goli contest, where you would get a car for hitting $100K GMV. 
> 
> These contests are great because they incentivize exactly what you want the creators to do, which is generate GMV. The only caveat is that it can lead to what I call "free ridership," where some of your top creators that were going to post anyways instead just get an additional commission for videos that they were already going to do that month. 
>  
> It is powerful though, just think about if you told your top sales person, “Hey, this month I'm going to double your commission." 
> They're definitely going to get a lot more prospects over the line, or at least they're going to try to. 
>  
> You just want to be careful here and make sure that it's actually incremental. 
>  
> Same reason I tell brands not to jack up commission rates on TikTok shops to like 40% or 45%, because I think the incremental number of creators that you get over just being at 30% is not worth the insane hit to the margins.
> 
> #3. A video-based contest. 
>  
> This is actually my favorite, even though it gets the most pushback and complaints from brands. 
>  
> This is a contest where you incentivize people for hitting a certain number of videos, so they might get $100 - $250 guaranteed for posting 10 videos, with a certain cap on how much the contest is going to spend before it closes. 
>  
> I really like these contests because, at the end of the day, GMV is downstream of the number of videos and content velocity, same as Meta.
> 
> The only caveat I would say here is to have some minimum quality guidelines where they have to post videos that are at least 30 seconds long, they have to actually demonstrate the product, it can't just be an unboxing, etc. etc.
> 
> If the video violates any of those rules, then you can disqualify the video from counting.
> This might get some creators upset, but it's honestly the only way to ensure that you're not just blowing money on really crappy content.
> 
> It also requires a decent amount of manpower because someone has to review everybody who qualified and their videos.
> 
> We have a system where we have a couple VAs that review all the winning videos manually before we start paying out. We tell creators that it's going to be net 30 payments, so we have a whole 30 days to just review everything and make sure that we're not paying anyone out that shouldn't be paid out.
> 
> Any questions on the hudson method or tiktok shop in general?
