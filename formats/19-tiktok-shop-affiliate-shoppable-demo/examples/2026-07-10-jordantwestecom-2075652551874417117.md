> If I was starting a TikTok Shop page today, this is what I would do👇
> 
> Pick the right hero products
> 
> I would not launch with the entire catalog.
> 
> I would choose 3 to 5 products that are easy to explain, easy to demo, have strong margin, and have enough inventory to handle volume.
> 
> TikTok Shop rewards products people understand fast.
> 
> If a creator needs 4 paragraphs to explain it, it is probably not the right first product.
> 
> Build the shop to convert
> 
> Before I sent any traffic, I would clean up the basics.
> 
> Strong product images.
> Clear titles.
> Simple descriptions.
> Accurate variants.
> Competitive pricing.
> Fast shipping.
> Clean offers.
> 
> The shop has to look trustworthy before creators start sending people there.
> 
> Set commissions high enough to matter
> 
> Creators are not influencers.
> 
> They are commission-motivated salespeople.
> 
> If the commission is weak, the best creators will skip you.
> 
> I would rather pay more to get momentum than protect margin on a shop nobody cares about yet.
> 
> Open the affiliate program early
> 
> I would get open collaboration live as soon as the shop is ready.
> 
> Then I would start recruiting creators every single day.
> 
> Not once.
> Not during launch week.
> Every day.
> 
> Creator recruiting is not a campaign.
> 
> It is an operating function.
> 
> Send samples with a system
> 
> I would not just send product and hope people post.
> 
> I would track every sample.
> Who requested it.
> Who was approved.
> Who received it.
> Who posted.
> Who converted.
> Who needs follow-up.
> 
> This is where a lot of brands lose momentum.
> 
> They seed product but never build the machine behind it.
> 
> Watch for organic winners
> 
> I would look for the first videos that get traction.
> Not perfect videos.
> 
> Videos that actually move people.
> 
> The hooks.
> The demos.
> The objections.
> The comments.
> 
> That is where the market tells you what it cares about.
> 
> Put paid behind what is already working
> I would not start by guessing with ads.
> I would let creators show me what works first.
> 
> Then I would use GMV Max to amplify the winners.
> 
> That is the play.
> Find signal.
> Then scale signal.
> 
> Treat TikTok Shop like a channel, not a side project
> 
> Someone needs to own the shop.
> Someone needs to own creators.
> Someone needs to own samples.
> Someone needs to own inventory.
> Someone needs to own reporting.
> 
> If everyone kind of owns it, no one owns it.
> 
> That is how TikTok Shop dies inside a brand.
> 
> The brands that win are not just posting more videos.
> 
> They are building the operating system behind the videos.
> 
> That is what I would do if I was starting a TikTok Shop page today.
> 
> Start small.
> Recruit daily.
> Track everything.
> Scale what works.
> 
> And do not send traffic to a shop that is not ready to convert.
