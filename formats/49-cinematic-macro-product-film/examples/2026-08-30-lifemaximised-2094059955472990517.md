## Full text

> Out top 4 YouTube ad formats printing the hardest right now, mapped to awareness stage and market sophistication:
> 
> (Prompts included. Bookmark this)
> 
> 1. Problem unaware buyers, low sophistication market
> 
> Format:
> 
> Claymation, 9 shots, 30 seconds (e.g. video 1)
> 
> Why the format works:
> 
> It’s a huge pattern interrupt from what we say day-to-day. This format resembles Wallace & Gromit, Pingu, Shaun the Sheep, etc triggering instant nostalgia. Plus, it feels handcrafted and high-quality.
> 
> Structure:
> 
> Introduce a relatable problem in the hook, then reveal a villain. Amplify how bad the villain is and how every typical solution doesn’t work. Show your hero ingredient, the mechanism at work, reveal your product that contains it, show a transformation in the avatar, reveal social proof, and end with a CTA.
> 
> Prompt:
> 
> "Write a 9-shot claymation YouTube ad script for [PRODUCT] following the problem-villain-hero arc. Each shot is one sentence under 20 words. Make the villain visually distinctive and slightly funny. Then write the GPT Images 2.0 prompt for each shot with lighting, camera angle, and clay texture direction that I’ll later animate with Seedance 2.5."
> 
> 2. Problem aware buyers, medium sophistication
> 
> Format:
> 
> Hybrid AI UGC, 30 seconds, talking to camera. (e.g. video 2)
> 
> Why the format works:
> 
> They know the problem exists and have likely tried something that failed. They need to see a person like them saying the product worked.
> 
> Key detail:
> 
> Use AI for the production elements such as lighting, b-roll, product showcase, and framing. But for the trust elements, including reviews and before-and-afters, try using real humans or at least don’t fake it too much. Fully synthetic UGC kills brand trust.
> 
> Fixing robotic voice and movements:
> 
> The biggest AI tell in UGC is the robotic voice. You can fix this by using slideshows, pasting a real human voice on top, adding an accent, writing the copy yourself, or using ElevenLabs. Similarly, to fix robotic movements, you can provide an example from a real UGC video and have AI copy the movements on top.
> 
> Prompt:
> 
> "Write a 30-second UGC-style script for [PRODUCT]. Structure: 3-second bold claim, 5-second problem using this exact customer language [PASTE REVIEW], 5-second discovery, 10-second proof, 4-second social proof, 3-second CTA. Include natural speech patterns, pauses, and filler words."
> 
> 3. Solution aware buyers, high sophistication
> 
> Format:
> 
> Cinematic product film, 15 seconds, no dialogue. (e.g. video 3)
> 
> Why the format works:
> 
> They have compared 6 brands. Creative quality alone can become your USP because every other ad is being ripped from another, and it’s probably all low-quality AI slop.
> 
> Prompt:
> 
> "Write a 15-second cinematic product film script for [PRODUCT]. Pure visual, no voiceover. 4 shots: macro texture detail, hero reveal in dramatic lighting, lifestyle context, CTA card. Then write the Seedance 2.5 animation prompt for each with camera movement and atmospheric audio."
> 
> 4. Remarketing at any stage (e.g. video 4)
> 
> Format:
> 
> Quick bumper, unskippable, YouTube Shorts placement.
> 
> Why the format works:
> 
> They already know you. You need frequency and recall intead of typical persuasion.
> 
> Shorts inventory is the most underpriced placement on Google right now because almost no one in eCom bids on it.
> 
> Match format to awareness stage.
> 
> Keep sophistication in mind.
> 
> These are the 4 highest-performing formats we’ve used to scale countless clients past $1M/mo on YouTube.
>
