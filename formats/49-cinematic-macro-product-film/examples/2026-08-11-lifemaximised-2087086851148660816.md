## Full text

> Here’s every element of my highest-ROAS YouTube ad format ever broken down into one tweet.
> 
> (Save this one, come back to it later when you need it.)
> 
> The hook (0-3 seconds)
> 
> The hook decides EVERYTHING.
> 
> You have 3 seconds before the skip button appears.
> 
> If the viewer's brain isn't locked in by then, you've paid for a view that converts nothing.
> 
> The winning hook structure: a bold visual pattern break combined with a text overlay stating the outcome the viewer wants.
> 
> "I cleared my acne in 14 days" over a close-up of someone's face, rather than "introducing our new skincare serum."
> 
> Viewers stay for their own problem being solved, and for nothing else.
> 
> The format
> 
> Claymation is printing hardest right now. Handcrafted stop-motion clay aesthetic. Warm, tactile, slightly jerky movement.
> 
> The internet is drowning in AI slop. Viewers have developed fatigue for hyper-glossy, obviously AI-generated content. Claymation triggers the opposite response. The brain reads the format as handmade and intentional. Real effort behind the frames. Even when AI-generated, the aesthetic signals work.
> 
> It has that Wallace and Gromit style that everyone loves.
> 
> YouTube and TikTok both shifted algorithms in 2026 to reward retention over engagement. Warm, cozy content retains. Claymation retains. CTR runs 2-3x feed average across our accounts.
> 
> The pacing
> 9 shots. Each shot lasts 2-4 seconds.
> 
> The arc follows problem, villain, hero, product, transformation.
> Shot 1: relatable problem the viewer identifies with
> 
> Shot 2: the villain (the biological mechanism or bad ingredient visualised as a clay monster)
> 
> Shot 3: villain escalation (stakes rising)
> 
> Shot 4: hero ingredient defeats the villain
> 
> Shot 5: HOW the ingredient wins (the mechanism at work)
> 
> Shot 6: product reveal
> 
> Shot 7: transformation (the character looks better)
> 
> Shot 8: social proof moment
> 
> Shot 9: CTA with offer
> 
> The villain is what makes this convert.
> 
> You're telling a story where the product is the WEAPON that defeats the enemy.
> 
> That narrative arc is why people watch past the skip button.
> 
> The CTA
> 
> One offer. One action.
> 
> Shown as a text overlay with the product hero shot underneath.
> 
> "50% off your first order" over the product image, instead of three paragraphs of copy and a URL nobody will remember.
> 
> The CTA appears in the LAST 3 seconds. Never earlier.
> 
> Every second before it is spent earning the right to ask.
> 
> The offer
> 
> An offer that isn't another 20% off.
> 
> When every ad in the feed says 20% off, the discount stops being a differentiator.
> 
> Gift with purchase, free shipping, subscription benefit, money-back guarantee, anything that breaks the pattern.
> 
> The brands printing $100k+ days all run offers that feel DIFFERENT from the category norm.
> 
> The creative earns the attention. The offer converts them.
> 
> The guarantee
> 
> "30 days to try, full refund if you don't love the results" removes the last objection standing between the viewer and the purchase.
> 
> The risk reversal converts the skeptics, and the skeptics are the majority of your high-intent audience.
> 
> Show the guarantee as a text overlay in the CTA shot, rather than buried in the landing page footer.
> 
> The targeting
> 
> Custom intent audiences.
> 
> People who recently searched your product category on Google.
> 
> Google KNOWS what people search for.
> 
> Target those searchers with a YouTube ad and you're reaching people who already have the intent.
> 
> You're intercepting researching buyers with a visual pitch instead of building awareness from nothing.
> 
> This is what makes YouTube fundamentally different from Meta for high-intent products.
> 
> The placement
> 
> The 6-second bumper on YouTube Shorts is unskippable and costs pennies per view. Almost nobody is bidding on that inventory.
> 
> Take your winning 30-second creative, cut the hook + CTA down to a 6-second bumper, and run it as a Shorts ad. The viewer gets your entire message with no skip option.
> 
> Shorts is priced way below what it's worth right now.
>
