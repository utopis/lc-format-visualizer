## Full text

> You can’t retarget abandoned carts if Meta can’t see the carts being abandoned.
> 
> That sounds obvious, but it gets missed more often than you’d think.
> 
> A brand may want to run abandoned cart retargeting, checkout abandonment retargeting, dynamic catalog ads, product-view retargeting, or other high-intent bottom-funnel campaigns.
> 
> But if the Meta pixel is not installed properly, the catalog is not connected, or events are not being passed back correctly, those campaigns are either impossible to build properly or too weak to trust.
> 
> Bottom-funnel retargeting starts before the ad is built.
> 
> It starts with the tracking.
> 
> Meta needs to see events like product views, add-to-carts, checkout starts, and purchases before it can build reliable audiences around those behaviours.
> 
> Dynamic catalog ads are a good example.
> 
> The whole point is to show someone the product they viewed, added to cart, or showed interest in. But that relevance depends on clean event data. Without it, Meta does not really know who saw what, who added what, or who should be pulled back into the buying path.
> 
> This is why tracking comes before campaign complexity.
> 
> A pixel that was installed yesterday may technically be active, but it still needs time and enough event volume before the account has meaningful retargeting pools.
> 
> And if campaign performance looks weak, the issue may not be the strategy.
> 
> It may be that the system is not seeing enough of the customer journey to execute the strategy properly.
> 
> The backend work is not glamorous.
> 
> Pixel events, catalog sync, attribution, audience building.
> 
> But that is the stuff that makes the more advanced campaigns possible.
