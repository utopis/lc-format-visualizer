> Secret of the DTC Universe #11: The Double 80/20 Rule in Creative Strategy
> 
> Okay so I've got another massive winner for that same eCom brand I did a batch of creatives for recently...
> 
> It’s my second massive winner for them in the past 21 days…
> 
> And this one is so strong that it’s actually cannibalized ad spend from my OG winner that got ~$750k ad spend in under three weeks.
> 
> Love seeing my ads rip…
> 
> But here’s the interesting part (and it’s the reason I’m turning this brag into a post):
> 
> When I was first onboarding, I was told the angle I used in this new winning ad “doesn’t work” for the brand.
> 
> They said:
> 
> “We’ve tried this in the past, but yeah, it’s never been a big winner.”
> 
> I told them:
> 
> “Okay, sounds good. I’m still going to try it in some creatives.”
> 
> Which I did.
> 
> And now it’s a behemoth that will probably do at least $1 million in ad spend over the next month or so.
> 
> So what’s the big takeaway here?
> 
> It’s that past performance is not indicative of future results.
> 
> Just because something hasn’t worked before doesn’t mean it will never work.
> 
> And a lot of the reason this is true comes back to what I’ve mentioned in other recent posts:
> 
> A winning ad is what happens when a strong idea meets great execution.
> 
> In the case of a yapper-style ad, great execution depends on a ton of variables like:
> 
> -The creator filming the video
> -The way they deliver the lines
> -The editing
> -The pacing
> -The captions on screen
> -The opening hook
> -The footage supporting the idea
> 
> If any one of those variables gets screwed up, a good idea can turn into a bombing ad.
> 
> –
> And this is why I don’t think you can permanently label an angle as a winner or loser without looking at how it was executed.
> 
> Sometimes an angle just doesn’t work for a market…
> 
> But sometimes the angle just hasn’t worked YET…
> 
> And in those cases, it’s because in past attempts…
> 
> The hook was weak, it wasn’t the right creator, the edit killed the pacing, the ad was poorly written and confusing, or something else.
> 
> —
> Now I want to be clear here…
> 
> I’m not saying you should ignore the brand every time they tell you something has failed before…
> 
> Instead, I’m saying that you should take more of a trust but verify approach.
> 
> And how do you verify?
> 
> You dig into the details and you get specific.
> 
> -How heavily did they test the angle in the past? Was it just a few ads or several?
> 
> -Did they test the angle in one batch of ads a single time? Or have they tried it numerous times over months or years?
> 
> -Did they try the angle in a variety of formats? Or were all the ads in the same format?
> 
> -What were the actual hooks? Were they good or weak?
> 
> -Was the angle a part of those hooks, or was it buried inside a weak ad?
> 
> Those details matter.
> 
> Because “we tried that angle before” doesn’t tell you much by itself.
> 
> —
> And if you’re reading all of this and nodding along going “yeah sounds good, but it sounds risky”...
> 
> Then this next part is for you.
> 
> It’s the part where I tell you how I personally go about retesting a “losing” angle…
> 
> And it comes down to what I’ll call “The Double 80/20 Rule in Creative Strategy.”
> 
> It goes like this:
> 
> **80/20 Rule #1:
> 
> Around 80% of your ads should use proven formats.
> 
> The other 20% should be more experimental formats.
> 
> **80/20 Rule #2:
> 
> Within the 80% of your ads that use proven formats, roughly 80% of those should use angles the market has already validated…
> 
> While the remaining 20% should be focused on testing new angles, and retesting old ones you believe in.
> 
> –
> 
> It’s not rocket science…
> 
> And it’s also not dogma.
> 
> So if you're reading this going "what if I want to test 75% proven ads instead of 80%?!?"
> 
> The answer is that yeah bro, that's fine...
> 
> I'm simply giving you a good rule of thumb that can simplify things for you…
> 
> And hopefully it helps.
> 
> -SPG
> 
> P.S. One extremely important caveat here.
> 
> It’s crucial you understand that we're talking about the ad level here.
> 
> This is because, in 2026, ads are where most of your testing should happen...
> 
> And it's where you can take bigger swings, try weird ideas, or retry previously doomed concepts.
> 
> What I am NOT saying though…
> 
> Is to take this post and go try and build a brand around some pain point or angle that everyone else has failed with.
> 
> That would be a horrible idea.
> 
> So for example - I’ve basically never seen brain fog work as the dominant pain point a product solves.
> 
> Doesn’t mean someone hasn’t cracked it (and I just don’t know about it)...
> 
> And doesn’t mean someone couldn’t make it work…
> 
> But trying to launch a whole new funnel for your supplement brand that’s focused on that pain point would not be wise.
> 
> Instead, if you really want to see if you’re the golden child who can get brain fog to work…
> 
> You’d test some ads for the supplement that speak to brain fog…
> 
> And, at most, if you needed to change a few words on your current product page, that’s fine.
> 
> If some of those ads randomly take off…
> 
> Then cool, now it makes sense to try creating some new landers, or advertorials, or quiz funnels or whatever that are focused on brain fog.
> 
> That’s fine…
> 
> Just start at the ad level first. Validate there. And then you can expand out to the other parts of your funnel.
