> Here’s an ugc example I created for Bucketlisters Nashville app
> 
> The goal was to showcase a limited-time 90s throwback bar while positioning Bucketlisters Nashville App as the place to discover and book unique experiences around the city
> 
> what I did:
> → opened vlog-style so viewers immediately felt like they were coming along with me to experience the pop-up
> → called out millennials directly since the 90s nostalgia was the strongest hook for the target audience
> → used TOSH right away to emphasize that the experience was only available for a limited time, creating urgency
> → showcased the fun throwback details throughout the space so viewers could actually see what made the experience worth booking
> → highlighted that a complimentary drink was included with the ticket to reinforce the value
> → made it clear that tickets for the experience were available through Bucketlisters Nashville, connecting the fun experience back to the platform
> → kept the content experience-focused so it felt like discovering something fun to do in Nashville rather than watching a traditional ad
> 
> why it worked:
> the creative combined nostalgia + urgency + discovery.
> 
> Instead of just showing a cool 90s bar, the content gave viewers a reason to act: it was limited-time, the ticket included a drink, and they could find and book the experience through Bucketlisters Nashville.
> 
> It also positioned Bucketlisters as more than a ticketing platform, it became the place to discover unique things to do around Nashville.
