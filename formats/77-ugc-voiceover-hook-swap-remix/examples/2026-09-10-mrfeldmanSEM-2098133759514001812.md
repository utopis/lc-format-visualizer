> How I split test creatives on YouTube without wasting budget:
> 
> → separate hooks from bodies before shooting anything.
> 
> - A hook is the first 5 seconds.
> - A body is everything after.
> 
> Test hooks against each other using the same body, rather than building 10 completely different videos.
> 
> 10 hooks times 2 bodies gives you 20 combinations from one shoot.
> 
> → launch with 3 to 5 creatives per ad group, not 10.
> 
> Too many at once and the algorithm never sends enough impressions to any single one to get a read.
> 
> 3 to 5 lets it find a winner in a reasonable timeframe without burning weeks of budget.
> 
> → wait 14 days before cutting anything.
> 
> The first week of data on a new creative is the worst it will ever look.
> 
> The algorithm is still figuring out who to show it to.
> 
> Cut at day 4 and you're cutting based on noise.
> 
> → cut on spend relative to your CPA, not on zero conversions.
> 
> A creative that's spent 2 to 3x your target CPA with nothing to show is done.
> 
> One that's spent $40 with zero conversions hasn't been given a real chance.
> 
> → check hook retention before you check conversions.
> 
> If people are skipping in the first 5 seconds at a high rate, the creative never had a chance to convert.
> 
> Fix the hook before blaming the offer or the landing page.
> 
> → when something wins, don't touch it.
> 
> Run the winner until it shows clear signs of fatigue: rising CPAs, falling retention, declining CTR.
> 
> Not because it's been running for 6 weeks and you're bored.
> 
> → produce in batches, not one at a time.
> 
> When a creative starts tiring, you want 3 to 5 new hooks ready to drop in, not a 2 week gap while you scramble to film something.
> 
> The brands that keep scaling YouTube are the ones who never let the pipeline run dry.
