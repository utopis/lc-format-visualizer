> Fully agree.
> 
> Most brands have a winning ad they haven't made yet and the footage is already sitting on a hard drive.
> 
> But there's a reason this works that most people miss.
> 
> Every winning ad is 3 layers:
> 
> Angle – the actual emotional reason someone buys
> Format – UGC talking head, static, podcast clip, meme
> Execution – the specific creator, hook, script, pacing
> 
> When an ad dies, it's almost never the angle.
> 
> It's the execution. The hook is stale. Everyone in the audience has seen that creator's face four times.
> 
> But the reason to buy is still true.
> 
> What Alex did was rebuild the cheap layer and keep the expensive one. Old footage, new DR script, same angle.
> 
> That's why it's top spender.
> 
> The instinct when a winner dies is to go find a brand-new one from scratch.
> 
> That's slow, expensive, and it throws away the one thing you already proved.
> 
> Here's how we rebuild instead, cheapest first:
> 
> 1. Use the winning ad itself
> 
> Don't touch the angle or the format. Remix the exact ad that worked.
> 
> – Same script, new creator. Tests words vs person.
> – Green-screen reaction. Shrink the winner into the background, new creator reacts in the corner.
> – Swap the hook. New 0–3s, same body untouched. Highest leverage move on the list.
> – Franken-cut two winners into one longer cut for warm audiences.
> 
> Zero new ideas. Four new tests.
> 
> 2. Same angle, new format
> 
> The reason to buy stays fixed. Repackage it.
> 
> – The winning UGC claim becomes one bold-text static.
> – Same claim, delivered as a two-person podcast clip. Borrows third-party credibility.
> – Same script, B-roll instead of a shoot, AI voiceover. Cheapest way to isolate whether the copy still works.
> 
> 3. Winning format, new angle
> 
> Keep the exact style and pacing. Point it at a different person.
> 
> Fast-talking rant-to-camera for busy mums who can't sleep → identical energy for women in menopause who can't sleep.
> 
> Don't guess the new angle.
> 
> Pull it from reviews, support tickets and post-purchase surveys. The customer already wrote it.
> 
> Two rules that make this a system instead of a favour to a dying ad:
> 
> 1.) Set a trigger.
> 
> Frequency over 2.5 and CTR declining means iterate now, before CPA blows up. Not after.
> 
> 2.) Never run one variant alone.
> 
> A single ad dying on its own is what causes the ROAS rollercoaster. Rotate 4–6 live iterations off every winner.
> 
> Ask one question when an ad dies: which layer is tired?
> 
> Execution → remix the ad.
> Format → new wrapper.
> Angle → new person.
> 
> You have more to work with than you think.
