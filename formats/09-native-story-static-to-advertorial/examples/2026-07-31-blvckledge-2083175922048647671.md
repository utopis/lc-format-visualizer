## Full text

> your pdp is not always the best place to send shopping traffic
> 
> advertorials are such an underrated way to scale shopping right now.
> 
> i know it sounds wrong but i'm seeing more and more brands printing with it.
> 
> a simple structure you can use:
> 
> 1. open with the problem or transformation they're looking for
> 
> 2. explain the hidden mechanism stopping them from getting there
> 
> 3. introduce your product and explain how it fixes that
> 
> 4. walk through the outcome they can expect, plus the benefits & unique selling points
> 
> 5. layer in testimonials, reviews, before/afters and other proof
> 
> 6. add a comparison chart against other solutions and competitors
> 
> 7. present the offer, guarantee, and CTA
> 
> 8. finish with an FAQ to handle objections
> 
> i’ve seen multiple brands running this with great success
> 
> these pages don't just increase your conversion rate.
> 
> they also let you address the buying psychology of different customers.
> 
> the skeptics, the comparison shoppers, those with more objections, etc
> 
> especially now that google gives you way more freedom with what images you use in shopping ads.
> 
> people are running:
> 
> - before-and-after (for some verticals)
> - close-up shot of someone applying the product
> - different avatars holding, wearing, or using the product
> 
> those angles pair really well with an advertorial.
> 
> which opens up a whole new segment of shopping traffic and revenue.
> 
> a few important notes on execution here:
> 
> optimize the page for mobile since that’s where a lot of shopping traffic comes from
> 
> drop the cta throughout the page (some traffic will be warmer and convert quicker)
> 
> match the page angle to the product titles, descriptions, and creatives
> 
> we’ve reviewed hundreds of ad accounts and 99% are using generic PDPs for shopping ads
> 
> adding an advertorial like this will instantly put you ahead of your competitors.

> next winning ad funnel for ecom brands:
> 
> shopping ad (trial offer) → PDP → quiz → cart (w/ upsell) → checkout
> 
> here’s a breakdown of a brand doing this really well, untamed:
> 
> many people think they have to use a product page for shopping ads.
> 
> but that's just one option.
> 
> truth is, you have a lot of flexibility in how you structure the funnel.
> 
> like in this case, the shopping ad leads with a low-priced trial product.
> 
> on shopping, price is one of the biggest things people compare between different products.
> 
> and a lower entry price gives you a big advantage over competitors
> 
> after the click, they land on the product page.
> 
> it covers all the basics:
> 
> - usp
> - social proof
> - features & benefits
> - a strong introductory offer
> 
> in my experience, this works better when the page is longer and spends more time answering objections & convincing people to buy.
> 
> after they click the offer, they go through a short quiz.
> 
> it recommends the best products based on their goals and what they're looking for
> 
> then they're given the option to upgrade to a full-size product or subscribe.
> 
> the whole funnel works like this:
> 
> use a low-priced trial to win more clicks where price has a big influence.
> 
> then use personalization to increase average order value
> 
> there’s a lot more you can do with google funnels than people realize.
> 
> from what i’ve seen, the majority of brands keep things very basic.
> 
> shopping ads straight to a PDP.
> 
> nothing wrong with that
> 
> but a few changes to the buying journey can give you a big advantage over competitors and massively improve your roi.
