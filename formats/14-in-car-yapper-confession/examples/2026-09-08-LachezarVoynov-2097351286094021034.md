> $300k/mo ad creative strategy:
> 
> We recently became the top-performing agency inside one of the ad accounts we’re working on.
> 
> At one point, our ads were spending $25k/day.
> 
> These are the steps we took:
> 
> 1. Ad account analysis
> 
> In most cases, the ad account already has the information you need to know in order to perform well.
> 
> - Audience demographic
> - Trigger events
> - Thousands of Facebook comments telling you exactly why people buy
> - Winning creative angles
> 
> It all comes down to how well you can identify winning ad creative patterns.
> 
> Pay attention to:
> 
> - the hooks of the top-performing ads
> - the narrative
> - the angle
> - the tone of voice
> - the pacing of the ad
> - the visuals
> 
> If you can identify exactly what makes someone purchase, it won’t be hard for you to create ads that spend $10k/day+.
> 
> Focus on the angle but try to connect it to the Life-Force 8.
> 
> LF8 are the 8 biological human desires that drive human behavior and decision-making.
> 
> Create a hypothesis and move on to the next step.
> 
> 2. Creative format identification
> 
> For me, this is the second most important variable.
> 
> Being able to identify ad creative formats that stand out in the feed but are also super relevant to your product, the problem you’re solving, and your audience is how you dominate the auction on Meta.
> 
> You can have the best DR script, the most detailed research, but if the wrapper - aka the Creative Format - is boring, you’ll lose.
> 
> For us, those wrappers were:
> 
> - Skits
> - Carpool ads
> - Suno songs
> - AI Pixar character podcasts
> 
> All of those are consistently becoming top spenders across many ad accounts we’re currently operating in.
> 
> 3. Hook testing
> 
> 99.9% of creative strategists get this very wrong.
> 
> The purpose of testing multiple hooks is not to just write the same idea in 3 different ways, so you can increase the amount of deliverables you ship to your client.
> 
> The purpose of testing different hooks is to have multiple entry points that would speak to completely different people.
> 
> Example: you’re selling a beauty product
> 
> Hook 1: before & after
> 
> Hook 2: introducing the desired outcome
> 
> Hook 3: starting with the problem people want to solve
> 
> Hook 4: talking about the failed solution they’ve tried in the past
> 
> You see how you will attract a different audience by having a diverse set of hooks.
> 
> It’s much better than:
> 
> - Hook 1: The sky is blue
> - Hook 2: I woke up and immediately saw the sky is blue
> - Hook 3: I called my mom, and she told me that the sky is blue
> 
> This is what most strategists do, and the biggest sign that you’re working with an under-qualified team member.
> 
> That’s how we currently produce winning ads for the brands we work with.
