## Full text

> You don't need to become more interesting to build a personal brand.
> 
> You need to start paying attention to what has already happened to you.
> 
> The customer conversation that changed your mind.
> 
> The product you almost built but killed.
> 
> The hire you got wrong.
> 
> The decision you made with incomplete information.
> 
> The mistake that cost you three months.
> 
> The assumption you had about your market that turned out to be completely wrong.
> 
> Those aren't just experiences.
> 
> They're content.
> 
> But most founders make one mistake:
> 
> They tell the story without extracting the thinking.
> 
> “We launched a feature and it failed.”
> 
> Interesting.
> 
> But not enough.
> 
> Try:
> “We launched a feature nobody used.
> 
> The obvious lesson was that we built the wrong thing.
> 
> The real lesson was more uncomfortable:
> We had asked customers what they wanted instead of understanding what problem they were actually trying to solve.
> 
> That changed how we build.”
> 
> Now you have:
> Story → Tension → Insight → Point of view
> 
> That's founder content people can remember.
> 
> Here's a simple test for any story:
> - Did something change?
> - Was there a decision?
> - Was something at stake?
> - Did you learn something?
> - Does it reveal how you think?
> 
> If you can answer yes to even 3–4 of these, you probably have a post hiding inside the story.
> 
> I've put 15 founder story prompts like these into my free Founder Personal Brand Playbook, along with frameworks for turning them into posts without making them sound manufactured.
> 
> Because your personal brand shouldn't be built from random content ideas.
> 
> It should be built from your actual experience.
>
