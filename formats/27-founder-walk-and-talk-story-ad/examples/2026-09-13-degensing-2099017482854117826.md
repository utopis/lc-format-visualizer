> How Personal Should a Founder Brand Get?
> 
> There’s a bad piece of advice floating around LinkedIn:
> 
> “Be authentic. Share everything.”
> 
> No.
> 
> You can build a powerful founder brand without turning your LinkedIn into a diary.
> 
> The real question isn't:
> “How personal should I get?”
> It's:
> “Does sharing this help people understand how I think?”
> 
> That gives you a much better filter.
> 
> 🟢 GREEN - Share freely
> 
> Things that make your expertise visible:
> → Business lessons
> → Customer insights
> → Product decisions
> → Hiring lessons
> → Mistakes
> → Industry observations
> → Principles you operate by
> 
> These are personal because you experienced them.
> 
> 🟡 YELLOW - Share carefully
> 
> Things like:
> → Difficult periods
> → Career uncertainty
> → Financial pressure
> → Major personal decisions
> → Conflicts
> → Experiences involving family
> 
> Ask yourself:
> “Am I sharing this because it helps the reader or because I need to release it?”
> 
> If it's the second one, don't post it.
> 
> 🔴 RED - Keep private
> 
> → Family privacy
> → Confidential customer information
> → Sensitive employee matters
> → Private financial information
> → Unresolved conflicts
> → Anything someone trusted you to keep confidential
> 
> Authenticity doesn't mean having no boundaries.
> 
> It means being honest about what you choose to share.
> 
> Here's the rule I use:
> 
> Be personal enough to be human.
> Not so personal that your brand becomes your diary.
> 
> And there's another important distinction:
> 
> Don't just tell people what happened to you.
> 
> Tell them:
> 
> What happened → what you thought → what you learned → what you believe now.
> 
> That's where a founder story becomes thought leadership.
>
