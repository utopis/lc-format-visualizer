## Full text

> This Eskiin ad is by far one of the best founder-led DTC ads I’ve seen lately.
> 
> And it’s not because the founders are charismatic.
> 
> It’s because the ad barely feels like an ad for the first half.
> 
> From a copywriting perspective, the structure is ridiculously smart:
> 
> It opens with a vision board.
> 
> A 16-year-old kid.
> One goal: retire his mom.
> Immediately, there’s an open loop:
> 
> How did he do it?
> 
> That question earns the attention Eskiin needs to tell a MUCH longer story.
> 
> Then they make an important move:
> 
> They don’t go straight to the product.
> 
> They go:
> 
> PERSONAL STORY → PROBLEM → FAILED SOLUTIONS → DISCOVERY → MECHANISM → PRODUCT
> 
> The founders struggle with eczema, dry skin, hair loss and rashes.
> 
> They try medications, shampoos and treatments.
> 
> Nothing works.
> 
> Then comes the reframe:
> 
> Could it be our water?
> 
> Now the viewer has a new explanation for an old problem.
> 
> And THAT is where the product becomes interesting.
> 
> The filtered showerhead isn’t introduced as another showerhead.
> 
> It becomes the logical conclusion of the story.
> 
> Then they systematically build the product around everything they hated about the alternatives:
> 
> Amazon filters? Cheap junk.
> 
> Existing filters? Weak pressure.
> 
> Expensive alternatives? Still not good enough.
> 
> So they build the thing they wanted themselves:
> 
> Better filtration.
> Strong pressure.
> Soft water experience.
> Clean design.
> 
> That’s classic problem → failed solutions → new mechanism → superior solution.
> But then the ad does something even better.
> It zooms back out.
> The story stops being:
> 
> We made a filtered showerhead.
> 
> And becomes:
> 
> We’re building the company we wish existed.
> 
> American fulfillment.
> No dropshipping.
> Great customer service.
> Listen to customers.
> Keep improving the product.
> Take care of employees.
> Take care of family.
> 
> Every product feature now doubles as a character trait of the founders.
> 
> Which matters because founder-led ads sell TWO things simultaneously:
> 
> The product…
> 
> and the people you’re trusting to make it.
> Then comes the payoff to the original open loop:
> December 2024.
> They retire their mom.
> The story finally closes.
> And only AFTER earning all that emotional investment do they transition into the guarantee + CTA.
> That sequencing is the key.
> 
> The ad doesn’t ask you to care about Eskiin.
> 
> It gives you reasons to care about two brothers first.
> 
> Then their problem.
> 
> Then their discovery.
> 
> Then their product.
> 
> Then their mission.
> 
> And finally their brand.
> 
> It’s essentially a mini-VSL disguised as a founder story.
> 
> Story creates attention.
> 
> Problem creates identification.
> 
> Failed solutions create dissatisfaction with the status quo.
> 
> The water reframe creates a new belief.
> 
> The product becomes the mechanism.
> 
> Customer results create proof.
> 
> The guarantee removes risk.
> 
> And retiring their mom gives the entire thing an emotional payoff.
> 
> That’s why this works so well.
> 
> The founder story isn’t decoration around the sales argument.
> 
> The founder story IS the sales argument.
