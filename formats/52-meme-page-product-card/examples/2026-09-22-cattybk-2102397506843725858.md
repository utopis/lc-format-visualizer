## Full text

> The most interesting companies being built right now aren't tech companies. They're ad agencies.
> 
> If, like me, you want to be creative but you're not talented enough to write a hit song or a blockbuster movie, you have to settle for working in media or advertising.
> 
> Both industries have their advantages.
> 
> Ad agencies are cheap to start, generate revenue from day one, and let you sell expertise and creative execution. They're also slow to scale, people intensive, and trade on relatively low multiples.
> 
> Media companies have a much higher ceiling, wield valuable influence, and compound quickly once you find meme market fit. They're more of a moonshot though, and consumer media is a graveyard (see Buzzfeed, Vice, etc).
> 
> What if there was a secret third thing? Why not both?
> 
> Meet the media studio.
> 
> There's a group of emerging media brands/ad agency hybrids who built an audience or a format first: a meme page, a curation account, a show, a newsletter - creator-led or faceless. 
> 
> Once the media gains traction, they launch a creative services team selling brands the knowledge and creativity that comes from building an audience. 
> 
> Revenue is split between selling ads on their own media and creative services, with the two endlessly feeding each other. 
> 
> In a world where the currency of brand building is attention, agencies who can prove they know how to capture attention, with an audience they own, have a huge advantage over agencies selling ideas and promises.
> 
> Distribution vs creative is a never ending debate. The studios with both have a massive advantage. 
> 
> Owning media also means you can test ideas for free with much lower stakes than working with a brand. As a result, agencies can shortcut virality by pitching an idea they've already proven has market fit. No more moonshots. 
> 
> Finally, media feeds the new business funnel, far more effectively than cold outreach or handshaking ever will. Content can be reverse engineered to land in the slacks of the brands you want to work with - and the execs picking agencies today are more aware of the new media landscape than they used to be. 
>
