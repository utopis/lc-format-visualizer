## Full text

> Here's how I tested incremental attribution (and a few other ways you can do it).
> 
> How I did it:
> 
> → New campaign, highest volume 
> → Duplicated my top 3 performing ad sets from my scaling campaigns  (standard scaling /creator whitelisting, and landing page) 
> → 3 ad sets total 
> → Switched the attribution on each ad set to incremental and let it run
> 
> Day 2 has seen a major improvement in CPA.
> 
> That said, this setup could be taking credit for conversions from other campaigns since they share ads.
> So keep that in mind before celebrating.
> 
> Here are a few ways to get around that (in theory):
> 
> 1. Make the complete shift
> 
> This is what Barry suggests and it's definitely the cleanest way. 
> 
> → Turn off all your campaigns and relaunch them with incremental attribution set on each ad set. The downside is you'll probably eat at least a day of bad performance while the new campaigns optimize.
> 
> 2. Use the A/B test tool
> 
> → If your structure is simple (i.e. one scaling campaign), you could duplicate the old scaling campaign and set incremental on the new one. Then use the A/B test tool to prevent audience overlap between the two.
> 
> One important note on this though. You're not trying to find a winner the normal way. The reported CPAs will be different on each because incremental may attribute fewer purchases (or more) than standard. 
> 
> To actually compare them, go to the columns dropdown, hit compare attribution settings, and select something like 7dc. Now both campaigns are being measured on the same yardstick.
> 
> I went the messier route because I wasn't ready to shut everything down. But early results have me paying attention.
