## Full text

> Everyone tests creatives, hooks and angles, and almost nobody tests the page the ad runs from.
> 
> That page changes how the whole ad gets read before anyone processes a word of it.
> 
> People clock who's showing them the ad first.
> 
> An ad from your brand page reads as advertising, because of course the brand thinks its product is good.
> 
> The same product coming from a different page reads more like content, or a recommendation from someone with no reason to lie.
> 
> Say you sell foundation under a brand called Glowé Cosmetics.
> 
> From the brand page it sounds like "our foundation is perfect for mature skin."
> 
> From a page called Beauty After 40, the same product becomes "I kept seeing women talk about this foundation, so I decided to see what the hype was about."
> 
> Identical product, completely different reception.
> 
> So you build a few pages and match them to your angles:
> 
> A content-style page for the educational stuff, running something like "3 foundations women over 40 are switching to."
> 
> A personal page for the story angles, where it's "I'm 52 and this is the first foundation that doesn't settle into my fine lines."
> 
> A review-style page for the comparison angles, testing the thing everyone keeps talking about.
> 
> A community-style page for social proof, along the lines of "women over 45 are ditching heavy foundation for this."
> 
> And your brand page for the direct, product-focused ads.
> 
> Now look at what that does to your testing.
> 
> You were testing creative by hook by angle.
> 
> Add the page and you've got another variable that meaningfully changes performance.
> 
> A creative that does average from your brand page can do a lot better from a content page, because the exact same ad is being received differently.
> 
> The rule is to match the page to the angle.
> 
> Education goes on the content page, personal stories go on the personal page, comparisons go on the review page, and product-led ads stay on the brand.
> 
> A founder story from a review page makes no sense, and a review from your own brand page fools nobody.
> 
> So test what people see in your ads, and test who they think it's coming from.
