## Full text

> this app went from $2k to $25k mrr in 4 months while running a Content Rewards campaign
> 
> $21,418 spent w/ 23.1m views.
> 
> the content was faceless AI slideshows on tiktok
> 
> if you're an app founder, here's what i'd actually take from this and apply:
> 
> -> give creators something they can execute today
> 
> “make content about our app” is barely a brief
> 
> this campaign had a vault of slideshow formats, a prompt bank, a Gemini tutorial and live calls
> 
> a new creator could join and understand what to make without spending 3 days asking questions
> 
> your brief should show a complete example: the opening slide, how the story develops, where the app comes in and what the viewer should do next
> 
> then explain what they can change
> 
> otherwise you get people copying the same post word for word, or making something completely unrelated to why anyone would download your app
> 
> -> expect most of the results to come from a small group
> 
> 139 creators submitted content here.
> 
> 60 had at least one post approved.
> 
> 10 generated 21m of the 23.1m views.
> 
> you don't know who those 10 are when you launch.
> 
> that's why you need to test different creators. but once someone starts working, actually pay attention to them
> 
> give them feedback, help them develop more angles, and make sure they have enough material to keep going
> 
> keep recruiting too. you want more people like them, not a campaign that depends on one person being available.
> 
> -> turn a winning post into more tests
> 
> the top creator posted 324 times and generated 6.2m views.
> 
> when something works, figure out what you can repeat.
> 
> was it the opening line? the situation people recognised? how the product was introduced?
> 
> keep that part and test a different story around it
> 
> if you change everything, you learn nothing. if you repost the exact same thing forever, you eventually run out of road
> 
> -> fund it in stages
> 
> they started with $4k and topped up over four months to eventually reach $21k
> 
> start with an amount you can afford to test, and use it to find promising creators and formats before increasing the budget
> 
> but don't top up just because a screenshot of the views looks good
> 
> check what happens after the view
> 
> i'd want to see which content brings people to the app, how many finish onboarding and how many pay
> 
> a post that gets fewer views but brings paying users can be worth a lot more to you
>
