## Full text

> Our 1.8k-follower UGC creator pulled 12.2M views while some 400k-follower ones can’t even hit 10k…
> 
> We’ve seen this happen across 100s of brands and the quality of the hook is the differentiator 99% of the time.
> 
> So here's my tier list of UGC hooks, ranked from worst to best, based on our campaigns:
> 
> D TIER: "Check out this new app I found"
> 
> - 0 tension
> - 0 curiosity gap
> - 0 reason to watch
> 
> This is what creators default to when the brief doesn't hand them a hook structure. 
> 
> Just way too generic and manufactured to succeed.
> 
> C TIER: the straight product showcase
> 
> "This app does X."
> 
> - Converts fine on the small audience it reaches
> - Almost never spreads
> - No one would send this to a friend
> 
> Fine as 20% of your output for a more BOF angle to test. 
> 
> But should never be the entire strategy.
> 
> B TIER: the industry callout
> 
> "Companies are scamming you with insane markups."
> 
> - Positions the brand as the one exposing the racket
> - Reliable performer
> 
> Capped though, because outrage hooks pull in people who want to be angry, and those aren’t always the ones who wanna buy.
> 
> B+ TIER: the comparison hook
> 
> "Why would I get a whoop when I can't even tell the time on it?" (then introducing Bevel, a competitor to whoop)
> 
> - 49.1K likes
> - 1,240 comments
> - On an 89 FOLLOWER account
> 
> Strong because it borrows the competitor's existing audience. 
> 
> Limited to categories with an obvious person worth punching at.
> 
> A TIER: the curiosity payoff
> 
> "discipline looks boring until you can see what it can build."
> 
> - 6,969 likes on a ~1K follower account
> - Entire video is 7 seconds
> 
> Promises a reveal in the first frame. 
> 
> The product IS the payoff, so the demo never feels like a pivot into an ad.
> 
> A+ TIER: the founder confession
> 
> "I personally fell for a scam, so I built this."
> 
> - 210.2K likes
> - 105.7K saves
> - 61.4K shares
> 
> Saves almost matching likes is absurd. 
> 
> People love to know what made you build the app in case it relates back to them.
> 
> S TIER: the secondhand embarrassment hook
> 
> "why did I let my bf play this with my family 😭😭"
> 
> - 5.1M views
> - 28.1K shares
> - 12% engagement rate
> 
> 28,000 people sent that to someone. The premise is funny before you even know what the product is, and the viewer NEEDS to see what happens next.
> 
> S+ TIER: the disbelief hook
> 
> "4 years at the gym and NOW I find this?!!!??"
> 
> - 12.2M views on a 1.8K follower account
> - Then 4.2M, 2.4M and 890K on reruns of the same hook
> 
> Works because it implies she genuinely didn't know the product existed until now. 
> 
> Nobody feels sold to, they feel like they're watching a real moment.
> 
> The "4 years" qualifier also establishes she's serious about this, so the recommendation carries weight.
> 
> What separates the top from the bottom
> 
> - S and A tier create an unresolved gap in the first frame, then make the product the thing that closes it
> - C and D tier announce the product first and hope somebody cares
> 
> Test to run on your next batch:
> 
> - Read ONLY the first line of text on screen
> - If it doesn't make you want the next 3 seconds, the video is dead
> - Kill it before the creator wastes an afternoon on it
> 
> Great hooks not only stop the scroll but also build curiosity and trigger an emotional response.
> 
> - Colin
