## Full text

> I talked to the CMO of a $10M+ ARR SaaS company. He broke down one of their best-performing ad hooks. It was based on 6 principles:
> 
> 1. Call out your exact ICP
> 
> @nielskle started by naming the exact tools his target customer was already using:
> 
> “If you're still using ClickFunnels or GoHighLevel…”
> 
> Then he called out the use case:
> 
> “…to build quiz funnels for your clients…”
> 
> In one sentence, he identified:
> 
> • The products they use
> • What they're using them for
> • The type of business they run
> 
> The more specific the callout, the more qualified the person who keeps watching.
> 
> 2. Call out the problem they already feel
> 
> He followed it with:
> 
> “I assume you like playing agency service delivery on hard mode.”
> 
> It's a jab, but it's intentional.
> 
> If you're an agency owner already frustrated by the problem, you immediately feel like the ad is talking to you.
> 
> 3. Remove the biggest objection before it appears
> 
> The ad then says:  “Give me 31 seconds…”
> 
> He's answering the viewer's next thought: “How long is this going to take?”
> 
> Then he immediately gives them the payoff:
> 
> “…and I'll show you how Perspective can shave 50% off your service delivery time.”
> 
> So within the first ~10 seconds, you've got:
> 
> ICP > Problem > Time commitment > Payoff.
> 
> 4. Rehook immediately
> 
> Then comes:
> 
> “By the way, if you watch until the end…”
> 
> And there's another payoff: successful funnel templates they can copy and paste.
> 
> So even after the initial hook, there's another reason to keep watching.
> 
> 5. Don't kill a winning creative; squeeze it
> 
> When they find a breakout winner, they don't just move on and make something completely new.
> 
> They turn it into Lego blocks:
> 
> • Same winning body + new hook
> • Same hook + new creator
> • Same idea + different environment
> 
> Their creative director will make 6-12 variations of a winner in the same week.
> 
> Sometimes a new combination becomes the next breakout winner.
> 
> 6. Stop assuming ads need to be short
> 
> ~80% of their video ads are longer than 1.5–2 minutes because they tested it and it worked.
> 
> For a product that needs education, a longer ad can actually qualify and educate the prospect before they ever reach the funnel.
> 
> And they deliberately start deep in the funnel.
> 
> Instead of trying to convince everyone they have a problem, they call out people already using competing tools.
> 
> They're targeting the 5-7% who are educated and ready to buy.
> 
> Then, once that audience is saturated, they broaden out.
> 
> This is how they can profitably spend $250K-$400K a month on ads.
