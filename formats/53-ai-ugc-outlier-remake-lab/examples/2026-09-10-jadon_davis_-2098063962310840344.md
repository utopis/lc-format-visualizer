> How the brands with the best results on SideShift run a first campaign, from someone who has onboarded a lot of them
> 
> 1. The brief fits on one screen. What the product does, who it's for, the feeling you want, the 2 things you legally can't say. Anything longer becomes a shot list and creators film shot lists like homework
> 2. Volume before taste. 50 to 100 creators, small budget each, paid on verified views. The first campaign is a search. The goal is finding out what your audience actually responds to, and one perfect video tells you almost nothing about that
> 3. Approvals in 24 hours. Every day a video sits in a queue it loses the trend it was filmed on. The brands who assign one person to approve same-day get more from the same budget than the ones who review as a group
> 4. Week 2 review on platform data only. Sort by verified views and conversions. Ignore the videos you personally like. Your taste is the least useful data point on the dashboard, and I say that as someone with opinions about every video that comes through
> 5. Rebrief the next batch on what won. Same hook structure, same format, different creators. This is where the outliers show up, bcs now you're searching a smaller space with real information
> 6. Scale the winners with spend. A creator video that hit keeps pulling installs for months while the platform's algorithm does your distribution for you. Ads stop when you stop paying. The winning videos don't
> 
> The whole thing is a loop, and the brands who get it treat every campaign as the research for the next one.
