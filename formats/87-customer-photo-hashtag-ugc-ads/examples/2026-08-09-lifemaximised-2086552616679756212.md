> Google Ads funnels are one of the biggest untapped opportunities in eCom right now.
> 
> A huge portion of your GADs performance comes down to JUST optimizing the landing page and everyone still ignores this.
> 
> So here’s how we structure them to make brands stand out and dominate their markets:
> 
> Above the fold (what the user sees WITHOUT scrolling):
> 
> This section has ONE job: confirm that the page matches the reason they clicked.
> 
> If they searched "sleep gummies without melatonin" and clicked your Shopping ad, the hero headline MUST say something about sleep without melatonin, in the exact words they typed.
> 
> "Premium Sleep Supplement" and "Natural Rest Support" both break the match instantly.
> 
> Above the fold contains 4 elements. No more:
> 
> 1. Headline that mirrors the search query or the ad copy they clicked on.
> 
> This is the message match. Break it and they bounce.
> 
> The tighter the match between keyword, ad headline, and landing page headline, the higher your Quality Score.
> 
> Higher Quality Score = lower CPCs = better ad positions = you outrank competitors while paying LESS per click.
> 
> 2. Subheadline that states the outcome with a timeframe.
> 
> "Fall Asleep in 20 Minutes, Wake Up Refreshed, No Melatonin Crash."
> 
> The outcome is the reason they buy. The timeframe makes the promise concrete.
> 
> 3. Hero image that shows the RESULT rather than the product.
> 
> Someone sleeping peacefully rather than a bottle sitting on a table.
> 
> The brain processes the image before reading the headline. If the image shows the desired state, the headline lands harder.
> 
> 4. 1 CTA button. 1 clear action, never 2 or 3.
> 
> "Try Risk Free" or "Get 50% Off Today."
> 
> The button text states the value of clicking rather than the mechanics of clicking.
> 
> "Add To Cart" is weak. "Get Mine for $19" is strong.
> 
> Below that fold, the page follows a specific sequence depending on the traffic type.
> 
> For transactional traffic (someone searching your product category, ready to buy):
> 
> > Trust bar (logos, review count, shipping guarantee)
> > 3 key benefits as icons with one-line descriptions
> > Social proof section (reviews, star rating, customer photos)
> > Product details (ingredients, specs, what's included)
> > FAQ addressing top 3 objections from your customer research
> > Final CTA with offer
> 
> For transactional traffic, the page is SHORT. These people are ready.
> 
> Don't make them scroll through 3,000 words of education to reach the buy button.
> 
> For informational traffic (someone searching a problem, not a product):
> 
> > Editorial-style hero (reads like an article rather than a product page)
> > Problem agitation section using the exact language from Reddit and customer reviews
> > "Why the usual solutions fail" (positions you against alternatives)
> > Introduction of your product as THE solution
> > Mechanism section (how and why the product works, briefly)
> > Social proof (reviews specifically mentioning the problem they searched for)
> > Comparison table (your product vs alternatives)
> > Risk reversal (guarantee)
> > CTA with offer
> 
> For informational traffic, the page is LONG.
> 
> These people need education before they're ready to buy.
> 
> The page does the work a sales conversation would do.
> 
> For comparison traffic ("brand X vs brand Y" searches):
> 
> > Hero: "How [Your Brand] Compares to [Category]"
> > Comparison table with criteria YOU win on
> > Key differentiators backed by proof
> > Testimonials from customers who switched
> > Offer with switching incentive
> > CTA
> 
> For comparison traffic, the page is MEDIUM length and visually structured.
> 
> The user arrived mid-decision.
> 
> Give them the decision framework that makes you win.
> 
> One landing page mistake that costs more than all the others combined:
> 
> Sending EVERY Google Ads campaign to the same product page.
> 
> Someone searching "how to fix gut bloating" needs a completely different page than someone searching "probiotic supplement 60 count."
> 
> One is problem-aware. The other is product-aware.
> 
> They need different copy, different structure, different proof, different CTAs.
> 
> The brands running angle-specific landing pages per campaign are converting at 2-3x the rate of brands sending everything to the generic PDP.
> 
> Build the page around the search that produced the click.
